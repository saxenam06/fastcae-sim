# The zenryx UI: from the contract to the datapoints (PLT-4, the web UI)

**Date.** 8 October 2026, 10:51. Asked for by the engineer at 10:38; written by Claude.

**Status.** Designed and built on 8 October (10:38-11:50), at the engineer's request ("plan the entire UI … and then build it"). It works locally. In PowerShell: `cd web`, then `npm.cmd ci` (on a fresh clone only), then `npm.cmd run dev`, then open http://localhost:5190. Nothing committed. Where it stands, and what is open: the [Platform page](status.md).

**What the engineer asked for, in short:**
- A UI built as the end-to-end product, not as a viewer of the current prototype's pictures. A trial made only to test something (the first variant) is not part of the product's flow, so it does not appear.
- It starts from the contract. Then it shows how each contract leads into campaigns, hypotheses, designs and grades, the agent's reasoning and next step, and how all of that becomes datapoints: decisions, the engineer's choices, counterfactual pairs, the four axes (task, attempt, evaluation, result).
- fastcae's UI is the model to copy: its design and its way of showing a pipeline.
- What exists now (the contracts) is shown. What the agent and the judge will make is laid out and left blank, to be filled when they are built.

---

## 1. Principles, carried over from fastcae's UI

1. **An engineering sheet, not a SaaS console.** Hairline rules, a quiet ground, white sheets, one blue; IBM Plex Sans and Mono; numbers in tabular mono. The tokens are copied from `fastcae-v1:ui/src/styles/tokens.css`.
2. **Every stage is shown, built or not.** A shell that hides its unbuilt stages describes a tool; one that shows them describes a product, and tells anyone looking where the work leads. A stage not built yet says so, and names what will fill it.
3. **Evidence is carried by line pattern as well as colour,** so it survives greyscale, screenshots and colour blindness. Here that is provenance: published, standard, measured, derived, assumption, engineer.
4. **Product UI, not documentation.** A few words an item, pills and toggles; the full text on hover or in the card on the right.
5. **Nothing on screen comes from anything but the records.** The UI reads one snapshot built from the repository's contracts. No number is typed into the UI.

## 4. How a contract is shown

**The rail** lists every contract with three dots for its stages (posed, derived, frozen: hollow until reached), whether the validator accepts it, and its hash when frozen.

**Binding (the default page): what binds a design**, in six boxes:
1. **Must carry, the duty:** torques by direction, speeds, power, ratio and its tolerance, the offset, the life, the gear train if given, the installation space.
2. **Must keep, the fixed interfaces:** bearing seats, split faces, mount pads, the motor flange, ports. A posed contract has none yet; its bearings are listed as the seats to come.
3. **Must meet, the verdict:** each limit (misalignment and centre-distance shares, ovalisation, bearing tilt by type, order separation at the check speeds, the model margin), the objective (least mass) and what is only recorded (stress).
4. **Must be makeable, the process:** the process, pull directions, drafts, the least wall, machining stock, the material.
5. **May vary, the agent's authority:** every entry the agent may move, with its bounds. Today none: every entry is engineer-locked, so the agent varies only the geometry inside the space.
6. **To confirm, the assumptions:** A1, A2 … with what, the value taken, and why.

Every value carries its provenance pill; a click opens it in the card.

**Sheet:** every entry by layer, as a table (what, value as written, canonical value, provenance, authority, kind), filtered by provenance with a chip per kind.

**Check:** the stage ladder (posed → derived → frozen, with what each step needs), every validator rule with *passed* or its refusal, and what freezing the contract now would say. The derived textbook case is ready to freeze; the two posed ones wait for the load tool (GBX-1).

**Sources:** every citation with the entries it supports; the standards; the assumptions; the engineer's own entries; derived values with their tool, version and input hash; and the published values kept as a plausibility band (references).

**JSON:** the contract as written, with its hash.

**The card** shows one entry in full: its layer and path, the value as written and in canonical units, where it came from (citation, tool and version, assumption with its reason, or who and when), who may change it (authority) and how it binds (kind).

## 5. How a campaign will be shown (blank now)

A campaign is one attempt at one frozen contract by one solver (the agent, the rule-based workflow, an optimiser or a person) with a budget.

**Steps** is fastcae's campaign grid turned to the agent: a step a row.

| Step | Saw | Hypothesis | Tools | Design | Evaluations | Grade | Status (code) | Next |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 7 | best 4.1 µm against 3.0; 62 % of strain energy at the pinion nose; 7 proofs left | "a web from the pinion nose to mount 1; ≤ 3 µm at +0.3 kg; wrong if < 10 % gain" | `hypothesise`, `reinforce_path`, `solve` ×2 | #8 | 3 static, 2 modal | pass, 2.7 µm, +0.25 kg | met | stop: verdict met |

(The row above is the data design's illustration, shown as the template; the page shows none until a campaign exists.)

**The step card is the decision record:** what the agent saw, what it decided and why, what happened. The stop rules (verdict met, the bound exhausted, the budget spent, every hypothesis set aside) are chips, the one that fired marked.

## 6. How the data are shown

- **Axes:** the four axes side by side. Task: the contracts. Attempt: campaigns, hypotheses, designs. Evaluation: the scenarios each contract implies (a static case per torque direction, the order checks at each check speed, mounted and free-free modes). Result: what came out. Under them, the test: does the engineer *require* it, does the judge *test under* it, or does it *come out*?
- **Datapoints:** kinds A-E with what each is, what it trains, how many exist and what makes them. A contract counts as a task datapoint once frozen.
- **Tasks:** the 20 task types of the data design, each with its grader and whether that grader exists yet (the validator and exact arithmetic exist; the load tool and the judge do not).
- **Pairs:** counterfactual pairs, and a comparison of any two contracts entry by entry. A counterfactual pair differs in one requirement; the comparison says how many entries two contracts differ in.

## 7. Where the data come from

- `src/zenryx/api/snapshot.py` reads the contracts (today the three examples in `checks/data/contracts/`), validates each, tries freezing it, and writes everything the UI shows to `web/src/records/snapshot.json`: `uv run python -m zenryx.api.snapshot`.
- The snapshot is deterministic (no timestamp) and committed. A check fails when it is stale, as for the example contracts.
- The records the agent and the judge will make (campaigns, episodes, candidates, evaluations, decisions, the engineer's choices, pairs) are empty lists. Their shapes in `web/src/records/types.ts` are provisional until the record schema (DAT-1) is agreed.
- **No server now.** The UI bundles the snapshot. PLT-5 (the API) will serve the same payload; the UI's `loadSnapshot()` then fetches it instead.

## 9. Checks, and done when

- `uv run pytest checks -q` runs the snapshot's checks; ruff is clean.
- `npm run build` in `web/` passes: the types check and the bundle builds. CI runs it as a second job.
- Every tab looked at in a screenshot of the built page (headless Edge, no server).
- **Done when** the engineer runs `npm run dev` in `web/`, opens `http://localhost:5190`, and sees the three contracts through every tab, with every stage not yet built visibly blank and naming what will fill it.
