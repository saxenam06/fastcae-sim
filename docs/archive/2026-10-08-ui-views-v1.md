# The UI's first version (archived 8 October 2026, 20:10 and 20:41)

**What this is.** How the UI's tabs looked from 11:50 to 20:35 on 8 October (PLT-4, the web UI).
- At 19:52 the engineer asked for the contract as one clean product page: "it looks like a documentation of a gearbox housing … we want to look at it like a product".
- At 20:35 the engineer asked for every other tab the same way: "build it like this".

The new pages are described in [the UI design](../features/platform/2026-10-08-ui-design.md), §3-8. The code of these views is kept in `web/archive/*-v1/`, outside the build.

## The other tabs, as they were

- **Flow:** a rail of the factory's eleven stages in four groups, each with its count, what made it and in/out chips. Two pages:
  - "Contracts through the factory": a chain of stage boxes, then a matrix with a contract a row and a stage a column;
  - "A campaign into data": a diagram of one campaign's steps feeding the decision records, snapshot tasks, preference pairs and graded designs, with the contract, the engineer's choice and counterfactual pairs beside it.
- **Campaigns:** a rail of campaigns by contract. Four pages:
  - Steps, with a template row and the stop rules and solvers as pills;
  - Hypotheses, with the statuses code gives and the six seeds;
  - Trace, with the tool table and the agent's tools in four groups;
  - Budget, an empty chart and each contract's budget.
- **Designs:** three pages:
  - Grid, a design a row and a stage a column, with each contract's verdict and the rungs;
  - Trade-off, an empty scatter;
  - Evaluations, each contract's scenarios.
- **Data:** a rail of the kinds, cuts and task types. Four pages:
  - Axes, the four axes with the scenarios each contract implies;
  - Datapoints, the kinds A-E and the splits by family;
  - Tasks, the 20 types with their graders;
  - Pairs, counterfactual pairs and a comparison of two contracts entry by entry.

## The contract views, as they were (until 19:52)

---

**The rail** listed every contract with three dots for its stages (posed, derived, frozen: hollow until reached), whether the validator accepted it, and its hash when frozen.

**Binding (the default page): what binds a design**, in six boxes:
1. **Must carry, the duty:** torques by direction, speeds, power, ratio and its tolerance, the offset, the life, the gear train if given, the installation space.
2. **Must keep, the fixed interfaces:** bearing seats, split faces, mount pads, the motor flange, ports. A posed contract had none yet; its bearings were listed as the seats to come.
3. **Must meet, the verdict:** each limit (misalignment and centre-distance shares, ovalisation, bearing tilt by type, order separation at the check speeds, the model margin), the objective (least mass) and what is only recorded (stress).
4. **Must be makeable, the process:** the process, pull directions, drafts, the least wall, machining stock, the material.
5. **May vary, the agent's authority:** every entry the agent may move, with its bounds. There were none: every entry was engineer-locked, so the agent varied only the geometry inside the space.
6. **To confirm, the assumptions:** A1, A2 … with what, the value taken, and why.

Every value carried its provenance pill; a click opened it in the card.

**Sheet:** every entry by layer, as a table (what, value as written, canonical value, provenance, authority, kind), filtered by provenance with a chip per kind.

**Check:** the stage ladder (posed → derived → frozen, with what each step needs), every validator rule with *passed* or its refusal, and what freezing the contract then would say.

**Sources:** every citation with the entries it supports; the standards; the assumptions; the engineer's own entries; derived values with their tool, version and input hash; and the published values kept as a plausibility band (references).

**JSON:** the contract as written, with its hash.

**The card** showed one entry in full:
- its layer and path;
- the value as written and in canonical units;
- where it came from: the citation, the tool and version, the assumption with its reason, or who and when;
- who may change it (authority) and how it binds (kind).
