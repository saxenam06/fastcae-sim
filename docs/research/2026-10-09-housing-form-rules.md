**Status:** written by a research subagent on 9 October, 12:58-13:24, as it went. **Scope.** Rules a parametric housing generator must follow so that what it builds looks and works like a housing an engineer would draw. First case: EV two-stage helical reducer, 150 N·m, aluminium, split across the shafts (motor-side half + cover). Already known and not repeated unless a source adds to it: TU Łódź (Maciejczyk & Zdziennicki 2011, Ch. 9, Table 9.1), Henkel's FIPG guide (flatness 0.1 mm/400 mm, 5 mm overlap, 3 mm round bolts, Rötscher-cone pitch), Ivanov 2018, Son 2019/2020, Kim 2024, Hexagon/GRM OED2023.

**Confidence.** High = read in the primary source by me this session; Medium = reputable secondary (lecture notes, a handbook quoted by others, a supplier page); Low = inferred or seen only in a search snippet.

## 1. Machine-element handbooks: cast gear-reducer housings

Three families of rules were found and read. (a) The Russian school (Dunaev & Lelikov's course-design book, Ch. 11, read in a web copy of the book; ITMO's 2014 housing guide, which restates Dunaev with a full proportions table; the NNGASU 2010 guide, which cites Dunaev 1971 and Sheinblit 1991). The Łódź handbook belongs to this school (its δ = 0.025·A + 1, d₂ = 0.75·d₁ and 2.35·δ are Chernavsky's), so these rules extend it rather than compete with it. (b) An Indian lecture (IIT Madras, NPTEL Machine Design II) with a wall table keyed to the housing's largest dimension L. (c) A German teaching page (INGGO TeachNet, MDESIGN) keyed to the largest clear internal length. All are for **horizontally split, cast-iron industrial reducers** (split through the shaft axes, cover on top). Our housing is split *across* the shafts and cast in aluminium, so the bolt and boss rules carry over directly, the wall formulas only as an upper reference (section 3 gives what EV housings actually use).

T below is the torque on the output (slow) shaft in N·m; d the cover-bolt diameter; δ the base wall, δ₁ the cover wall; D the bearing's outer diameter.

## 2. Design for casting, specific to housings

General casting values (draft, least wall, machining stock) are left to the parallel note `casting_rules.md`. This section keeps to the housing's *structure*: where ribs go, how they end, how they meet bosses, and what flat panels do acoustically.

**What this gives the generator.** Every rib is rooted on two features (boss to flange, boss to boss, boss to mount, mount to flange), never ending in the open; it runs along the straight line between its two features (the force path); it is thinner than the wall it sits on (≈ 0.8·δ), as tall as the package allows (height is the cheap stiffness and the cheap noise cut), filleted and drafted; ribs on the two faces of a wall are staggered; and each rib's own first bending mode is a design quantity (its free area and outer edge), checked against the orders at cruise.
