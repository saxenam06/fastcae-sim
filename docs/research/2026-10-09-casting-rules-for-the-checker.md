# Casting design rules a castability checker can enforce (research, 9 October 2026)

For: zenryx, cast aluminium gearbox housings (EN AC-42100 T6 / A356-type, sand cast, walls about 4-8 mm).
Already known and not repeated: NADCA 2015 die-casting draft constants (2° inside / 1° outside for features at least 25.4 mm deep), die-cast walls 1-5 mm, NADCA machining stock 0.25 mm; the TU Łódź reducer handbook (δ = 0.025·A + 1 ≥ 7.5 mm, bosses 1.2·D, flange 2.35·δ); Henkel's FIPG bolt pitch; Magnecast and Batesville (permanent mould) rules in fastcae's 2026-10-06 AM report, section 5.4.

Confidence: High = read in the primary source; Medium = a reputable secondary source quoting it, or a primary source read only in part (search snippet, abstract); Low = inferred.

**Status:** written by a research subagent on 9 October, 12:58-13:31, as it went. It stopped at the session's usage limit, after its three topics' tables and its URLs. ## 1. Aluminium sand casting with a conventional pattern

| Rule | Value | Quote | Source | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Uniform walls; taper a necessary thick-to-thin change | Qualitative: keep walls consistent; where thick meets thin, taper, never step | "always attempt to keep all wall thicknesses consistent ... if you can't avoid this, make sure you taper the wall from thin to thick, avoiding the abrupt section change" | D. Charbauski, "Four rules to ensure a smooth casting process", *Casting Source* (AFS), May/June 2025 [U1] | High |
| Junctions (T, L, X, Y) and bosses/ribs make the heavy sections | Qualitative: each junction kind has its own design rule; isolated heavy sections must be reachable by a feeder | "An isolated heavy section is any area of a casting that cannot be easily reached with a riser ... The resulting wall connections are defined by their shape; a "T", "L", "X", or "Y" section all have specific guidelines" | as above [U1] | High |
| Core prints: support and vent every core | Qualitative | "proper core support keeps the core in its correct location ... The core prints are also vital as they provide a vent path where core gasses ... can escape" | as above [U1] | High |
| Fillets: always, but not so large that they make a heavy section | Qualitative | "A fillet will eliminate this condition, but be sure that it is not too large and creates a heavy section." | as above [U1] | High |
| Hot spots at heavy masses and at cored-in regions | Qualitative: uniform thickness; core out heavy sections ("adding geometry to the core") | "Abnormally thick masses of metal and internal areas substantially surrounded by sand cores will cool at a slower rate ... A good casting design limits the number of junctions and avoids hot spots and sharp corners." | "Designer's Sketchpad: Hot spots and sharp corners", *Casting Source* (AFS), Jan/Feb 2020 [U2] | High |
| Cross-ribbing (ribs on both sides of a wall): stagger it | Stagger the rib junctions by **at most one rib thickness** to limit the strength loss | "Cross-ribbing ... is undesirable ... It also creates hot spots ... The loss in strength and the amount of metal used can be held to a minimum at the intersections by staggering the rib junctions less than or equal to the thickness of the rib." | "Designer's Sketchpad" (ribs), *Casting Source* (AFS), Nov/Dec 2021 [U3] | High |
| Ribs: compression better than tension; thin ribs fail | Qualitative; a bead (rib on the parting plane) rated best for load | "Ribs in compression while in service offer a greater factor of safety than ribs in tension. Thin ribs are subject to failure in service." | as above [U3] | High |
| **T-junction thickness ratio (rib or wall on a wall): the thermal rule** | Leg t on a plate d: **t << d = cold junction (fin); t = d/2 neutral; t = d hot spot ("must always be avoided"); t = 2d neutral.** Fins are "especially useful" for local cooling in aluminium | "If we now jump to junction C where we have two legs of equal thickness (d = t) joined together. This creates a hot spot ... This type of junction must always be avoided. ... when one plate of thickness d is joined to a plate of half of that thickness, the cold and hot junction effects are balanced to give a neutral junction." | J. Campbell and R. A. Harding, *TALAT Lecture 3206: The Feeding of Castings*, EAA (Training in Aluminium Application Technologies), 1994, slide 3206.00.04 and text p. 6-7 [U4] | High |
| Feeder modulus (Chvorinov) | **M_f ≥ M_c, normally M_f = 1.2·M_c**; modulus = volume / cooling area: cube d/6, long bar d/4, plate d/2 | "the modulus of the feeder (Mf) equals 1.2 times the modulus of the casting (Mc)" | TALAT 3206, rule 1, slide 3206.00.02 [U4] | High |
| Feeder on a casting (junction requirement) | Avoid the junction (extend the casting and feed the extension); if not, **M_f = 2·M_c** (in practice perhaps 1.5·M_c), "not ... 1.2 Mc" | "Rule 3.1 is to avoid the creation of junctions, particularly T junctions. ... Rule 3.2 is to ensure that the modulus of the feeder is twice that of the casting ... it is important not to use Mf ~ 1.2 Mc" | TALAT 3206, rule 3, p. 7 [U4] | High |
| Feed path | A thin section that freezes first cuts a heavy section off its feeder: pad, add a feeder, chill, or change the design | "There must be a path to allow feed metal to reach the regions that need it." | TALAT 3206, rule 4, slide 3206.00.05 [U4] | High |
| Aluminium's feed demand | Volume shrinkage of aluminium ≈ 7 %; with a typical sand feeder (ε ≈ 14 %) the yield is about 50 % | "α for aluminium is about 7% ... aluminium has a very high feed metal requirement since the yield ... is only 50%." | TALAT 3206, rule 2, p. 5-6 [U4] | High |
| "Do not feed unless necessary" | Campbell's zeroth feeding rule; the seven feeding rules: heat transfer, volume, junction, feed path, pressure, pressure gradient | "Do not feed unless absolutely necessary!!" | TALAT 3206, slides 3206.00.11-13 [U4]; the ten rules: J. Campbell, "Casting Practice: Guidelines for Effective Production of Reliable Castings", *ASM Handbook* Vol. 15 (2008), abstract [U5] | High (TALAT); Medium (ASM, abstract only) |
| Hot tears sit at hot spots; Al-Si resists them | Qualitative: Al-Si alloys (A356 family) are not easily hot-tearing because of their eutectic liquid; Al-Cu alloys are | "Tears are often located at hot spots." ... "Such alloys include the Al-Si family and their hot tearing resistance explains the popularity of these alloys as casting alloys." | J. Campbell, R. A. Harding, *TALAT Lecture 3207: Solidification Defects in Castings*, EAA, 1994, slides 3207.00.21 ff. [U6] | High |
| Thin ribs cool a wall; thick ribs make the hot spot (simulation) | On a 20 mm sand-cast wall: ribs **< 0.5 × wall** shorten solidification (best at 0.15 ×: −12.5 %); the effect is gone above 0.45 × wall; ribs ≥ 0.75 × wall give one hot spot on the rib's axis | "the cooling effect (or wing effect) is observed in the case of ribs thinner than 0.5a (half the wall thickness)" ... "for rib thickness exceeding bn = 9mm the cooling effect of the ribs disappears" | I. Ciobanu, D. Ţuţuianu, T. Bedő, A. Crişan, "Influence of Rib Thickness on the Solidification of Cast Parts", *Annals of "Dunarea de Jos" Univ. of Galati*, Fasc. IX, 33(4):24-32, 2010 (eutectic cast iron in silica sand, Sim-3D) [U7] | High (read); applies to aluminium only by analogy |

## 2. Sand moulds and cores made by 3D printing (binder jetting)

| Rule | Value | Quote | Source | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Pattern draft: not needed (confirmed)** | No draft or undercut limit from the mould or core: nothing is drawn from the sand. Draft may still be put where the mould pieces mate (assembly), see below | "A design engineer has more freedom in designing parts and does not have to consider limitations such as drafts or undercuts." | K. Woods, S. Ravi, "Design Considerations for 3-D-Printed Cores and Molds", *Modern Casting* (AFS), 15 May 2015 [U9] | High (as read through the page fetcher) |
| Draft and undercuts drive conventional tooling cost (why printing removes them) | Qualitative | "major limitations in mold making using traditional techniques ... include constraints such as limitations on minimum wall thickness, elimination of sharp corners, and undercuts resulting in higher draft angle" | E. S. Almaghariz, B. P. Conner, L. Lenner, R. Gullapalli, G. P. Manogharan, B. Lamoncha, M. Fang, "Quantifying the Role of Part Design Complexity in Using 3D Sand Printing for Molds and Cores", *Int. J. Metalcasting* 10(3):240-252, 2016 (open access), p. 241 [U10] | High |
| Draft on the mould's own mating features | Draft the joints between printed mould pieces so they assemble; keep to one parting line so the cavity can be cleaned | "It was also recommended to add draft angles ... to the connection between the two pieces of the mold to aid with the assembling of the mold." ... "Feedback recommended to decrease the number of parting lines to one." | J. Rodriguez et al. (UTEP, with ExOne), "Mold Design using Binder-Jetted Sand for Aluminum Casting", *Solid Freeform Fabrication Symposium 2024*, pp. 732-751 [U11] | High |
| **Least feature (wall) of the casting in a printed mould, A356** | **4 mm** (ExOne's guidance to the UTEP team; thinner features risk misruns) | "the minimum thickness of any feature should not be less than 4 mm ... Any features within mold designs with a thickness smaller than 4 mm can lead to improper flow of liquid metal, leading to a potential failure of the casting." | as above, p. 733 [U11] | High (one project's guidance, small part) |
| Unbound sand must come out of every core and cavity | Design a path for compressed air or vacuum; horizontal cutting planes were not enough; contour-offset parting lines gave access | "Provide or design a method to remove the uncured sand from within the cores themselves, often using compressed air or vacuum." | Woods and Ravi, *Modern Casting* 2015 [U9] | High |
| Non-horizontal parting lines for sand removal; vents printed in | Qualitative: vents placed from the filling simulation are printed into the mould; risers anywhere without removing undercuts; cores consolidated | "Enhancement of mold design through novel nonhorizontal parting lines to allow for un-bonded sand removal"; "Simplification of vent fabrication (i.e., elimination of vent incorporation into hard tooling)" | Almaghariz et al. 2016, p. 241 [U10]; Woods and Ravi 2015 [U9] | High |
| Closed cavities and holes in a printed sand body | Holes ≥ **3 mm** diameter; a closed cavity keeps its powder unless a **30 mm** hole is drilled; printed wall ≥ **5 mm**; details ≥ 1 mm; build box 1000 × 1800 × 700 mm (quartz sand) | "Minimum diameter of holes: 3 mm" ... "In closed cavities, the powder remains in the component. If powder is to be removed, drill a 30 mm hole." | jellypipe (Swiss print bureau), "Binder Jetting technology (BJ)" page, sand and PMMA [U12] | Medium (a bureau's rules, for printed sand bodies; not specific to cores in castings) |
| Blind areas, sharp edges, handling | Few blind areas (cleanable and inspectable); obtuse rather than acute sand edges; rods or steel plates through large prints for handling | "Too many of those increase your risk of a bad casting, so design it so it can be seen and cleaned." | D. Rittmeyer (Hoosier Pattern) in S. Davies, "Sand 3D printing: the dos and the don'ts", *TCT Magazine*, 10 Sept 2019 [U13] | Medium (practitioner, interview) |
| Machine envelope and layer | Printers up to 4 × 2 × 1 m, layers 280-500 µm (2016); ExOne S-Max Pro 1800 × 1000 × 700 mm, layers 0.2-0.5 mm, ±0.5 mm | "print volume up to 4 m × 2 m × 1 m with layer thickness in the range of 280-500 microns" | Almaghariz et al. 2016, p. 241 [U10]; ExOne S-Max Pro datasheet (search snippet) [U14] | High (paper); Medium (datasheet snippet) |
| **What printed moulds are used for, and volumes** | Cost-effective below **≈45 castings** for low-complexity parts and up to **≈1000** for complex ones (2016 costs); prototypes and small series. Series exception: BMW Landshut prints cores for **six cylinder-head variants, up to 4,500 cores a day on 17 printers** (inorganic binder) | "3D sand printing is shown to be cost-effective for low quantities (<45 parts) of castings with lower complexity. However, it can also be very cost-effective for casting with higher complexity even at quantities of 1000 units." / "The plant makes up to 4,500 units on 17 printers daily" | Almaghariz et al. 2016, abstract [U10]; BMW Group press release "BMW Group makes further investment in Plant Landshut", 18 April 2024 [U15] | High |
| Decoring cast-in cores from aluminium | Aluminium's low heat input leaves cores strong: more effort to get the sand out of internal passages (worse with inorganic binders) | "For aluminium alloy castings, due to the lower heat load achieved, the more effort is usually required to remove the sand cores from the castings." | T. Obzina et al., "Evaluation of Collapsibility of Selected Core Systems", *Archives of Foundry Engineering* 2022(1):48-52 [U16]; F. Ettemeyer et al., "Characterisation of the decoring behaviour of inorganically bound cast-in sand cores for light metal casting", *J. Mater. Process. Technol.* 296:117201, 2021 [U17] | High (read) |

## 3. Aluminium high-pressure die casting

NADCA's *Product Specification Standards for Die Castings* (2021 edition), read in full from the copies of Sections 4A and 6 that a member die caster (A&B Die Casting) publishes [U18, U19]. The fillet figure was read from the rendered page, since its text layer is partly garbled.

| Rule | Value | Quote | Source | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **L-corner, equal walls (T1 = T2)** | Inside radius **R1 = T1**, outside **R2 = R1 + T1** (the wall stays uniform round the corner; the figure says "without limit" for this case). With a sharp outside corner (R2 = 0), R1 = T1 to 1¼·T1 (that line of the figure is partly garbled in the PDF) | "IF R2 = R1 + T1; R1 = T1" (figure G-6-2, Fig. 1) | NADCA 2021, Section 6, guideline G-6-2 "Fillets in Die Cast Parts", p. 6-4 [U19] | High (Fig. 1 first line); Medium (the R2 = 0 line) |
| **T-junction** | **R1 = T1 to 1¼·T1**, T1 = the thinnest metal at the junction | "TEE JUNCTION R1 = T1 TO 1¼ T1" ... "WHERE T1 = THICKNESS OF THINNEST METAL AT JUNCTION" | as above, Fig. 2 [U19] | High |
| **L-corner, unequal walls (T2 > T1)** | **R1 = ⅔·(T1 + T2)**; R2 from 0 up to R1 plus the wall (the figure prints "R1 + R2") | "R1 = ⅔ (T1 + T2)" | as above, Fig. 4 [U19] | High (R1); Medium (R2) |
| **Cross or Y junction, by the angle θ between the legs** | θ = 90°: all radii = T1; θ = 45°: R1 = 0.7·T1, R2 = 1.5·T1; θ = 30°: R1 = 0.5·T1, R2 = 2.5·T1. R1 is the fillet **inside the acute angle** (the smaller one), R2 the one on the obtuse side (the figure prints the second "R1" for R2) | "Θ = 90°; ALL RADII EQUAL T1 / Θ = 45°; R1 = 0.7 T1 R1 = 1.5 T1 / Θ = 30°; R1 = 0.5 T1 R1 = 2.5 T1" | as above, Figs. 5-6 [U19] | High (values); Medium (which radius is which, read from the drawing) |
| Thickness transition | An angular (tapered) transition between T1 and T2 over a length shown as 2·(T1 + T2) "increases strength but costly die work" | "ANGULAR TRANSITION INCREASES STRENGTH BUT COSTLY DIE WORK." | as above, Fig. 3 [U19] | Medium (the length read from the figure) |
| Fillet neither too big nor zero | A fillet too large makes a heavy mass (unsound metal); one approaching zero cannot be held in the die; no fillet = "weak casting" | "HEAVY MASS HERE NOT CONDUCIVE TO SOUND METAL." | as above, Figs. 8-11 [U19] | High |
| Fillets carry draft; deep pockets want larger fillets | A fillet normal to the parting line takes the draft of the wall it joins; shallow castings may have smaller fillets, deep pockets and inside corners larger | "Shallow die castings may have much smaller fillets, while deep pockets and other inside corners should have larger fillets." | as above, p. 6-4 [U19] | High |
| **Least radius** | Metal savers and clearance holes **≥ 1.5 mm**; functional/locating holes **≥ 0.8 mm**; "as large a radius as possible, consistent with uniform wall thickness" | "Consider 0.06 inch radius (1.5 mm radius) as a minimum." | NADCA 2021, Section 4A, "Design Recommendations: Cored Holes As-Cast", p. 4A-25-26 [U18] | High |
| **Ribs** | A bead or rib of height h = T1 may be solid; **where h > T1, core out underneath**; a rib from flange to boss with "generous draft and fillets"; ribs inside a boss housing = good; external ribs, sharp roots and little draft not recommended. No rib-thickness ratio is given | "WHERE h > T1 CORE OUT UNDERNEATH TO AVOID UNDESIRABLE HEAVY MASS OF METAL." | NADCA 2021, Section 6, G-6-3 "Ribs and Corners in Die Cast Parts", p. 6-5 [U19] | High |
| Rib spacing: the die's metal savers | Avoid deep, sharp-edged or little-drafted metal-saver pockets between close ribs (thin die steel) | "Often, adding ribs close together can result in thin or weak metal savers required in the die cast die to form the rib features in the part." | as above [U19] | High |
| Uniform wall round holes | Wall round metal savers, clearance and functional holes **within ±10 %** of the typical wall | "Try to maintain wall thickness within ±10% of the most typical wall section." | NADCA 2021, 4A-25-26 [U18] | High |
| **Wall stock round a cored hole** | Holes < 6.35 mm (0.25 in): wall stock ≥ **½ the hole diameter**; larger holes: the nominal wall; rib before thickening | "For holes with larger than a 0.25 inch diameter, the wall stock shall be the nominal wall thickness (subject to part design)." | NADCA 2021, 4A-26 (side note) [U18] | High |
| **Cored hole depth, aluminium** (recommended maximum) | Ø3.2 mm → 7.9 mm deep; Ø4.0 → 12.7; Ø4.8 → 15.9; Ø6.35 → 25.4; Ø9.5 → 38; Ø12.7 → 51; Ø15.9 → 79; Ø19 → 114; Ø25.4 → 152 (depth/diameter 2.5 rising to 6) | "Depths are recommended maximums and are not necessarily the limits for a specific die caster." | NADCA 2021, 4A-26, "Hole depths" table [U18] | High |
| Cored holes for tapping | Tables S-4A-9/P-4A-9 give D1, D2, thread and hole depth per thread (e.g. M8 × 1.25: D1 ≤ 7.281, D2 ≥ 6.825, thread ≤ 18.00, hole ≤ 21.75 mm); Al may be tapped without removing draft | "cored holes in Al, Mg, Zn and ZA may be tapped without removing draft" | NADCA 2021, 4A-28-29 [U18] | High |
| **Slides (moving die components) for undercuts** | Each dimension formed by a slide gets a "plus" MDC tolerance on top of the linear one, rising with the slide head's projected area: aluminium **+0.20 mm up to 64.5 cm²**, +0.61 mm at 484 cm² (NADCA's example); draft on every surface parallel to a slide's motion | "Draft should be added to any surface parallel to die/tool movement" | NADCA 2021, 4A-11, table S-4A-3 [U18] | High |
| Pressure-tight parts | Uniform walls; core holes and passages rather than machine them; ample draft in unmachined cored holes; avoid heavy sections and abrupt changes; little machining, never both sides of one section | "Holes and passages requiring pressure tightness should be cored to reduce porosity, as opposed to machined after casting." | NADCA 2021, Section 6, G-6-1, p. 6-3 [U19] | High |
| Draft, die casting (die caster) | ¼° to 1° per side | "A draft angle between ¼ to 1 degree per side is generally recommended" | Dynacast, "Design Tips for Improved Die Casting" (web article, undated) [U20] | Medium |
| Rib thickness ratio in HPDC | 0.4-0.6 × wall, height 2-3 × wall: stated by service bureaus and blogs only, unattributed; these are injection-moulding numbers | (not quoted; no primary source found) | Xometry, Wevolver and similar (search snippets) | Low |
