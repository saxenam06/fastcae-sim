| 2026-10-13 | [2026-10-13-autodesk-curvature-and-interface-preserving-to.md](2026-10-13-autodesk-curvature-and-interface-preserving-to.md) | The engineer's question of 13 October 00:03 (how to penalise the surface's rate of change round the regions that matter, so the optimiser stops wrinkling them). A reviewer proposed a region-weighted curvature penalty on the height field, Σ w (h_xx² + h_yy² + 2h_xy²), citing two Autodesk Research links. **They are one paper:** Humphry, Ebrahimi, Morris & Butscher, *Computers and Structures* 319 (2025) 107973. It is a level-set method that holds each interface to rigid motions and aligned scalings by a hard restriction on the update velocity (the constrained Hilbert space extension), beside frozen and free regions. It has no curvature or accessibility constraint, no weights and no graded strengths. Its only smoothing is of the update velocity (γ, set by hand). It reports kinks where constrained and free regions meet, proposes an untested second-derivative energy for them, and reports no cost in stiffness. The curvature-and-accessibility claim fits a different Autodesk paper, Morris, Butscher & Iorio 2020 (SMO, milling): a hard [0, 1] mask on the boundary speed sized by the tool, a least concave radius equal to the tool's, at 1.37-1.52 × the unconstrained compliance. For us: the squared Hessian is the right form (zero on planes) but not on ribs; a per-region bound in mm rather than weights; hard subspaces we already have (`topo.volume_map`); brackets need a mechanical requirement.
